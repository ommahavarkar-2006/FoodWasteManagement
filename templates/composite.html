🌱 Composting Guide — Simple & Effective Tips

Turn kitchen scraps into nutrient-rich soil — reduce waste, help your garden, and fight climate change.

1. What Is Composting?

Composting is a natural process where organic waste (food scraps, leaves) breaks down into nutrient-rich soil that improves plant growth and keeps waste out of landfills.

🍃 2. What You Can Compost (Green Waste)

  • Fruit peels
  • Vegetable scraps
  • Coffee grounds & tea bags
  • Bread, rice, and grains (small amounts)
  • Fresh grass clippings
  • Expired veggies or fruits

🍂 3. What You Should Not Compost

  • Meat, fish, eggs
  • Dairy products
  • Oily or greasy food
  • Plastics or metal
  • Diseased plants
  • Pet / animal waste

⚖️ 4. Composting Formula: 50% Green + 50% Brown

Greens (Nitrogen): food scraps, coffee grounds, grass clippings.
Browns (Carbon): dry leaves, shredded paper/cardboard, sawdust.

🤲 5. How to Start Composting at Home (Step-by-Step)

  1. Choose a bin: bucket, compost drum, or simple pile.
  2. Layer browns first: dry leaves or cardboard.
  3. Add food scraps: daily vegetable/fruit peels in small pieces.
  4. Add soil or old compost: seeds the process with microbes.
  5. Mix weekly: turn to supply oxygen.
  6. Keep moist (not wet): water lightly if too dry.

⏳ 6. How Long Does Compost Take?

Under good conditions compost can be ready in 2–3 months. Time varies with temperature, particle size, and how often you turn it.

🪴 7. Benefits of Composting

  • Reduces household waste
  • Creates natural fertiliser for plants
  • Improves soil health and water retention
  • Reduces methane emissions from landfills
  • Saves money on chemical fertilisers
